How MSM Rating Works

Raw times tell you who ran fastest. MSM Rating tells you who ran best — accounting for the course, the field, and the weather that day.

The problem with raw times

A 16:20 on a flat, fast course on a cool October morning is not the same effort as a 16:20 on a hilly course in 85°F heat against a stacked state-meet field. Raw-time rankings reward easy conditions. The MSM Rating normalizes every performance to a level playing field so runners across different meets can be compared fairly.

Both views live on the rankings page. Toggle Raw Time to see pure results, or MSM Rating to see the adjusted, condition-normalized ranking. Nothing about the raw data is changed.

1 Start with pace

Every performance is first reduced to pace (seconds per meter), so a 5K, a 2-mile, and a 3K can all be compared on the same footing. Faster pace → higher rating.

2 Adjust for three real-world factors

The raw pace is then divided by three difficulty factors. Each is a multiplier: above 1.0 means "conditions made this harder," which pushes the rating up to give the runner credit.

⛰️

Course Difficulty

We compare the median pace everyone runs on a course to the statewide median. Courses where the whole field runs slow (hills, footing, elevation) earn a credit. Built purely from your own results — no manual rating needed.

🏁

Field Strength

Beating a deep, fast field is worth more than winning a small one. We measure how strong each race's field was (median pace and depth of finishers) relative to a typical WV race.

🌦️

Race-Day Weather

We look up the actual weather for each meet's date and location from Open-Meteo's historical archive — heat, wind, and rain all slow runners down and are credited back.

adjusted_pace = raw_pace ÷ (course × field × weather)

3 Convert to a rating & a prediction

The adjusted pace is mapped to a rating on a ~1000-point scale. A rating near 1000 is a solid varsity performance; higher is better. Because the rating represents effort on a neutral course in neutral weather, we can invert it into a tangible projected neutral 5K time — shown under each rating as "proj 5K 16:04." That projection is the model's best estimate of what the athlete would run under fair, standard conditions.

rating → projected neutral 5K time (your "true" 5K)

Worked example

Two runners both post a raw 16:30 5K on the same day:

RunnerRaw TimeCourseWeatherFieldMSM Rating
Runner A16:30Flat, fast (0.97×)Cool, calm (1.00×)Small (0.99×)~985
Runner B16:30Hilly (1.06×)82°F, windy (1.07×)State meet (1.05×)~1120

Same clock time, very different performances. Runner B ran the harder race and the MSM Rating reflects it.

How teams are scored

In Raw Time view, teams use the classic cross country place-sum (top 5 finishers, lowest total wins). In MSM Rating view, a team's score is the sum of its top 5 athletes' ratings — higher is better — so a team that raced tough courses and deep fields isn't penalized for a slow clock.

🏟️ MSM Rating for Track

Track is different from cross country. Every event is contested on a standardized surface — a 400m oval, a runway, a throwing circle — so there's no course-difficulty factor to compute. Instead, the track MSM Rating scores each mark against the elite standard for that exact event and gender, which puts a 100m dash, an 800m, and a shot put all on the same ~1000-point scale.

The scale

A mark equal to the elite (state-champion caliber) standard scores about 1000. Beating the standard scores higher; falling short scores lower. Because running events reward smaller numbers (faster) and field events reward larger numbers (farther/higher), the model flips the math for field events so "higher rating = better performance" always holds.

rating = 1000 + (percent vs. elite standard) × 1000

What still applies

🏁

Field Strength

Winning a loaded invitational is worth more than winning a small dual. We measure how strong and deep each meet's field was for that event and give a modest bonus (clamped to ±6%).

🌦️

Outdoor Weather

Outdoor meets get a race-day weather check from Open-Meteo — heat, wind, and rain are credited back. Indoor meets are climate-controlled, so no weather term is applied.

📏

No course factor

Unlike cross country, a track is a track. There is no terrain adjustment — the elite standard is the level playing field.

Toggle Raw Mark for pure results, or MSM Rating for the standard-adjusted score. Raw marks remain the source of truth and drive the default view.

Limits specific to track

  • Standards are tunable. The elite benchmarks per event are set in the page's ELITE_STANDARDS table. If ratings sit too high or low for an event, adjust that one number.
  • Weather needs meet dates & venues. Outdoor weather only works where a meet carries a date (and ideally lat/lon). Missing either, the weather term is skipped and field strength still applies.
  • Cross-event comparison is directional, not exact. A 1050 in the 800m and a 1050 in the discus are both "excellent," but the model isn't a scientific decathlon-style conversion.

Honesty about the limits

  • Derived factors need data. A course or meet with only a handful of results gives a noisy factor, so we clamp adjustments (roughly ±10%) to avoid wild swings.
  • Weather needs coordinates. We map each course to a lat/lon; where a course location isn't yet mapped, we fall back to a state-center estimate or skip the weather term and rely on course + field adjustments.
  • It's a model, not a stopwatch. The projected 5K is an estimate. Raw times remain the source of truth and are always one toggle away.
